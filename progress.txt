2026-07-29 building your own moons

DONE
- A ☾ button on any body in a system you own rolls a moon into orbit
  around it, and an Orbits selector moves any world between the star
  and any planet. A moon's distance is measured from its planet and
  quoted in that planet's radii, which is how moons are actually
  quoted.
- satPeriodFor: a moon's year from its planet's size. Mass goes as the
  cube of radius at a given density, and splitting gas giants from rock
  at their real density ratio reproduces the Moon within 0.4% and
  Jupiter's moons within 3%. Saturn's come out a third quick because
  Saturn is half Jupiter's density — a fair trade for one constant, on
  systems that were invented anyway. Measured satellites keep their
  measured years and never go through it.
- Note for anyone reusing periodFor: it floors mass at 0.02 solar
  masses because it was written for stars, so it returns nonsense for
  planets. satPeriodFor does the arithmetic directly.
- Guards, all unit-tested: moons of moons refused at both ends (you
  cannot add a moon to a moon, and a planet carrying moons cannot
  become one — the selector disables itself and says why); removing a
  planet returns its moons to the star with a real distance; changing
  what a world orbits re-derives its distance, since the old number was
  measured from somewhere else.
- Drawn band corrected: the floor was clearing the planet's radius but
  not the moon's own, so a user-built moon sat slightly inside its
  planet. Floor raised to 1.55x and the unit test now asserts surface
  clearance rather than centre distance. A second test pins that the
  band never collapses to zero width — stacked moons are worse than
  crossing an orbit line, which same-size mode does anyway.

  re-timing the orbits blanks the size mode and resizes the star again.
