2026-07-29 the Safari re-profile, and what it overturned

DONE
- bun run perf:engines: a portable profiler over WebKit and Chromium.
  It measures the largest gap between animation frames instead of long
  tasks, because WebKit has never implemented the Long Tasks API — its
  supportedEntryTypes are event, first-input, largest-contentful-paint,
  mark, measure, navigation, paint, resource, and nothing more. Every
  long-task number this project has recorded describes Chromium alone.

MEASURED (M4 Pro, three runs per engine, medians, same production build)
- WebKit / Apple GPU            stall  31 ms, 0 frames over 50 ms
- Chromium headed / ANGLE Metal stall  38 ms, 0 frames over 50 ms
- Chromium headless / SwiftShader stall 353 ms, 3 frames over 50 ms
- Identical 55,920 triangles in all three, so the scene is the same and
  only presentation differs.

THE FINDING
- On the hardware this app runs on, the long task does not exist. A
  31-38 ms gap IS the passive cadence: 30 fps is a frame every 33 ms.
- The 353 ms stall belongs to SwiftShader, the software rasteriser
  headless Chromium falls back to with no GPU — which is the
  environment every benchmark here has ever run in, local and CI.
- This reverses the standing hypothesis. The attribution work reasoned
  that a long task starting 0.2 ms after first render implied driver
  shader compilation on Metal. The opposite is true: real drivers are
  fast enough to be invisible and software is the slow one. Shader
  readiness is 13-17 ms everywhere, SwiftShader included, so it was
  never the cost either.
- Cold-start ruled out: against a fresh profile with an empty GPU
  pipeline cache, Metal gave 38 ms and zero frames over 50 ms —
  indistinguishable from warm. There is no first-visit penalty.
- The original success criterion, no main-thread task over 50 ms, is
  met on real hardware in both engines by a wide margin, and probably
  has been for some time. The tooling could not see it.

NEXT
- longTaskMaxMs/longTaskTotalMs stay as relative regression detectors
  on a consistent runner, which is what the CI profile and noise floors
  already make them. They are not user-facing numbers and the 600 ms
  ceiling describes SwiftShader's first draw.
- The one measurement still outstanding is Safari itself rather than
  its engine: safaridriver needs an administrator and cannot run
  headless. WebKit and Chromium-on-Metal agree within 7 ms, so a
  disagreement would be a surprise.

2026-08-01 the Moon, properly

DONE
- A moon is never drawn smaller than it is. The sub-linear size curve
  is now only a floor, which is all it was ever for — Phobos and
  Deimos are subpixels at their true size — and above it the number is
  the measurement. Our Moon goes from 0.09 of Earth to its real 0.273;
  Charon from a pebble to the half of Pluto it actually is.
- The single-world camera frames the whole moon orbit, derived from
  the field of view. The old rule multiplied the orbit by 1.32, which
  bears no relation to a 45-degree view: at a distance d you see 0.41d
  either side of the middle, so two thirds of every orbit was off
  screen and the Moon was mostly not there. Earth is smaller in frame
  for it. That is the trade, and it is the right way round.
- In the orbit view a moon's year is tied to its planet's rather than
  to the wall clock. Each planet's moons share one factor, slowed
  together until the fastest of them is watchable, so the ratios
  inside a family stay exact. The Moon now laps Earth about eleven
  times a year against a true thirteen. It used to manage one lap
  every four Earth years, which is what "not orbiting" looked like.
- Tidal lock in the orbit view. Every satellite in the tables is
  locked and says so — sidereal day equals orbital period — so the
  drawn spin is read off the drawn orbit instead of running on a
  second clock. The two are compressed differently, so the old
  independent spin drifted: the Moon showed Earth every face it has,
  several times a lap.
- The lock is now visible, which on a plain grey sphere it never was.
  The Moon wears the measured albedo difference — anorthosite
  highlands at 13 per cent against basalt seas at 7 — turned toward us
  and held there. Iapetus's dark half moves to its leading hemisphere,
  which is where it comes from.
- Moons cast shadows. A moon between the sun and its world drops a
  real one on it, and the orbital node precesses, which is the reason
  eclipses arrive in seasons instead of once an orbit. Measured over
  50 lunar orbits: about one eclipse every two, from a graze at the
  limb to dead centre.

WORTH KNOWING
- The deep part of an eclipse is brief, and that is the geometry
  rather than a setting. The Moon is drawn 4.25 radii out, so the
  window where its shadow lands anywhere on the disc is 27 degrees of
  the orbit, and the part where the shadow is well inside the disc
  rather than grazing the limb is half of that — about two seconds of
  a 57-second lap. Come in at 4x or 20x if you want to watch one
  cross; at 1x it is a thing you catch, which is the right feeling.
- Only the moons cast. The world casting back would be a lunar
  eclipse and worth having, but the world is the tens of thousands of
  triangles in this scene and a shadow map means drawing every caster
  a second time each frame. A moon is a 22-segment sphere.
- Gas giants do not receive. Their bands come from a hand-written
  shader that samples no shadow map, so Io's shadow does not cross
  Jupiter. That is a shader to be written, not a decision taken.
- Not PCFSoftShadowMap. Three deprecated it: asking for it earns a
  console warning and a silent swap to PCFShadowMap on the first
  shadow render, and that swap recompiles every material in the scene
  mid-frame. It left the cloud shell sampling a shadow map that was
  being replaced underneath it and painted Earth's weather black.
- Moon distance stays compressed and always will be. The Moon is 60
  Earth radii away; drawn there it is off screen with Earth a dot.
  Everything else about the pair is now the measurement — size ratio,
  period ratio, lock, shadow — but the gap between them is a frame
  constraint, not a number.
- The eclipse signal published for tests counts events rather than
  reporting the current state: an eclipse is over inside a tenth of an
  orbit, so anything sampling on a timer would mostly find nothing and
  conclude that shadows never fall.

FIXED IN PASSING
- Every regeneration after the first — a time scale, a toggle, a
  slider — slammed the single-world camera back to 3.15 whenever the
  world had moons. The framing branch only runs when the set of moons
  changes; every later pass fell through to the else and reset. A moon
  system framed itself once and lost it at the next keystroke.
- A moon's ascending node did nothing. Rotation order applies X last,
  so the node was the inner rotation of the two and only shifted the
  moon's phase inside a plane every moon of that planet shared. It
  turns the plane now, which is what makes precession mean anything.

2026-08-01 the sky from where you are standing

DONE
- A display option, Sky, draws the real sky in the single-world view: the

  to 1.73 degrees across as its eccentric orbit carries it; Earth's holds
  between 0.525 and 0.542; Pluto's runs from 0.018 down to 0.011. A disc
  at the true size with a bloom around it, the bloom a fixed multiple of
  the disc, so a small sun glows small.
- The planets as points of light, because that is what they are. Jupiter
  from Earth is forty arcseconds — a fiftieth of the Moon — and every
  planet in every system here is under a minute of arc. They carry their
  own colour, and a brightness read the way a magnitude is: reflected
  sunlight, falling off with distance twice, dimmed by the phase turned
  away. Four decades below the brightest is the bottom of the scale.
- Names come from the Labels option that already existed. This is the one
  place a label is not decoration: without it the sky is honest and
  illegible, a true-size planet being a point, and a point among fourteen
  hundred invented stars is not a planet anybody can find.
- The whole sky is turned by the one rotation that lands its star on the
  light. Every angle inside it is then the true one, measured from a sun
  that is still wherever the light sliders put it. What that leaves free
  is the roll about the sun's own axis, and nothing depends on it.
- A moon observes from its planet. Ours is a fifth of a percent of the
  way to the Sun, which moves nothing in that sky by a pixel — and the
  planet itself is left out, the view already drawing it beside the moon

2026-08-01 latitude bands: obliquity and circulation

CONTEXT
- The v2 terrain generator landed with its own climate model, and it is
  the better one: per-preset albedo, greenhouse, atmosphere, biosphere
  and frost point for 22 worlds, eccentricity, tidal heating for moons,
  a habitable zone, and a piecewise mass-luminosity relation. A parallel
  climate module written here was discarded rather than merged; it would
  have clobbered better work with worse.
- What v2 was missing was the two things that decide latitude structure,
  and those are ported onto it here rather than alongside it.

DONE
- Obliquity that can invert. The pole gradient was 42 K scaled by
  sin(tilt), which is monotonic and can never make a Uranus. It is now
  scaled by the second Legendre coefficient of annual-mean insolation
  over Earth's own value: exactly 1 for an Earth-tilted world, so an
  ordinary world is drawn exactly as it was, zero at 54.7 degrees, and
  negative beyond, where a pole receives more light over a year than the
  equator and the arrangement of a planet inverts.
- Circulation cells from the rotation rate, off the Held-Hou scaling for
  the width of a Hadley cell. Earth's day gives three, Venus's 117 days
  gives one, Jupiter's ten hours gives five. Moisture alternates every
  90/n degrees, so the dry belt lands at 30 degrees.
- Descending air now suppresses rain as well as failing to source it: a
  belt keeps only 56 per cent of what the wind carries into it. Without
  that the bands could only ever add water, and a belt that is merely
  less wet is not a desert.
- Tilt and day ride on OrbitalClimate, which is already threaded
  everywhere, so nothing new had to be plumbed. Both are in the canonical
  key: they are geography now, and a re-tilted or re-timed world compiles
  again rather than reusing a cached terrain that assumed otherwise.

WORTH KNOWING
- The dry belt is asserted in the compiled terrain, not just in the
  helper: land moisture in the 25-35 degree band comes out lower than at
  the equator and lower than at 50-70. A slow world's belt disappears, as
  it should, and a world on its side comes out warmest at its poles while
  an upright one stays coldest there.
- Nothing in the code names 30 degrees. It is where three cells put the
  descending air, and it is the Sahara, the Arabian, the Kalahari, the
  Atacama and the Australian.

  tagged sRGB — so the sampler hands it linear light — then wrote that value

  very same texture on a standard material, which round trips correctly, so one
  planet had two appearances. Added the two output chunks.
- `solidTexture` wrote THREE.Color's linear components into an sRGB-tagged
  texture, so every placeholder landed a stop or two darker than the colour
  asked for. Encoded on the way in.
- Orbiting worlds now get the single view's surface treatment. The flat bake can
  return clouds as their own layer and leave the albedo bare, and the orbit node
  hangs them on a shell; compositing them in had turned cloud cover into a white
  wash over the ground, which is most of why a cloudy world read pale in orbit
  and saturated up close. The relief normal map comes from one shared pass used
  by both artifact kinds, so the bumps are the same bumps at either resolution.

FIXED ALONG THE WAY
- Materials in the scene graph are no longer disposed while a shader warmup may
  still be reading them. `compileAsync` keeps the material set it prepared and
  polls each program for readiness on its own timer; disposing one drops the
  renderer's record, so the next poll throws inside three's timer where nothing
  can catch it and the promise never settles at all. Rebuilding the bodies is
  exactly that disposal and it runs on every edit. `retireMaterial` holds them
  until the warmup is done. This was pre-existing — the compile deadline was
  added for it — and it cost about 160 ms on every orbit transition.
